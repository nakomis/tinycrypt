# tinycrypt key board (CRYPT-16)

The JLCPCB-assembled key: ESP32-S3-MINI-1-N8, ATECC608B, WS2812B-2020, a button and a JST-SH
socket for a captive USB cable. The board is generated by code, from the circuit to the gerbers.
`DESIGN.md` has the parts and the circuit.

## Build

```bash
ASDF_PYTHON_VERSION=3.11.15 python -m venv .venv
.venv/bin/pip install -r requirements.txt
mkdir -p freerouting && cp <somewhere>/freerouting-2.5.0.jar freerouting/   # Java via asdf (25)
./fetch_parts.sh                                       # only if footprints/ is missing or a part is added
.venv/bin/python build.py --board key --route          # full log: /tmp/claude-501/key-board-route.log
.venv/bin/python -m pytest                             # includes one full route (about 15 s)
```

`build.py` writes `out/key/`: `tinycrypt-key.kicad_pcb`, `drc.json`, `bom.csv`, `cpl.csv`,
`tinycrypt-key-gerbers.zip`, the top and bottom renders, and `pins.h`. It writes the fab files only
when KiCad's DRC has no errors and no unconnected items (kicad-cli exits 0 even when DRC fails).
Freerouting isn't deterministic, but it now routes only BUTTON, LED_GPIO and LED_DIN, which are short.

## 3D model (for the case)

```bash
.venv/bin/python build.py --board key --route --step   # also runs ./fetch_models.sh (resumable)
```

`--step` fetches the parts' 3D models (STEP + WRL, about 17 MB, into `footprints/lcsc.3dshapes/`,
gitignored), then writes `out/key/tinycrypt-key.step` (about 9 MB, gitignored; a 1.6 mm board with
its holes and every part's body) and an isometric render, `out/key/tinycrypt-key-3d.png`.

- Every placed part has a body. The WS2812B-2020-V6 has no EasyEDA model, so it uses the WS2812B-2020
  one (C965555): the same 2.0 × 2.0 × 0.82 mm package with the same pad layout.
  The test pads, holes and art have no body.
- `board/parts.py` `MODEL_OFFSET` corrects easyeda2kicad's offsets where they're wrong, found from
  renders: SOT-23-6 and C0603 bodies sat off their pads, the JST body was 2 mm to one side, and the
  capacitors were half inside the board.
- Heights above the board's top face: J1 2.9 mm (the tallest), module 2.4, U4 (SOIC-8) 1.7,
  U3 (SOT-23-6) 1.6, SW1 1.5 (its plunger top), U2 1.1, D1 0.82, C0603 0.8, C0402 0.5, R0402 0.4.
  Nothing on the back, so the bottom is the bare 1.6 mm board.
- The module's antenna overhangs the board edge by 5.04 mm in the model (5.05 mm in the datasheet).

## Pins (for the firmware)

`out/key/pins.h` holds these; `board/key_board.py` (`GPIO`) is the source.

| Signal | GPIO | Module pad |
|---|---|---|
| Button (presence, download strap) | 0 | 4 |
| USB D- / D+ | 19 / 20 | 23 / 24 |
| ATECC608B SDA / SCL (4k7 pull-ups) | 38 / 21 | 34 / 25 |
| WS2812B DIN (through 330R) | 17 | 21 |
| UART0 TX / RX (debug pads) | 43 / 44 | 39 / 40 |

J1 (JST-SH, viewed from the top with its opening facing you, pin 1 on the left):
1 VBUS, 2 D-, 3 D+, 4 CC, 5 GND. Debug pads TP1, from the antenna end: **EN, RX, TX, G, 3V3**.
Bridge EN to G to reset the chip.

## Ordering (JLCPCB)

First order, 16 Oct 2026: 5 boards, all assembled, about £110 with shipping, VAT and baking.

- **Standard PCBA**, not Economic: Economic can't place the ESP32-S3-MINI-1 (pads underneath; X-ray
  inspected) or the 2020 LED, and it allows neither OSP nor yellow. JLCPCB then adds its own edge rails
  (the panel becomes 71 × 71 mm).
- **OSP** finish (cheapest; flat enough for the module), yellow solder mask, white silkscreen (the
  only one on yellow, so Mu is faint; black shows her best). 1.6 mm.
- The BOM matcher may leave U1 (and D1) unticked with quantity 0: tick them. D1 (MSL 5) is baked
  before reflow (accept the pop-up).
- Check the rotations of J1, D1, U1, U2, U3 and U4 in the placement preview. D1 has no 3D model, so
  compare its DI/−/+/DO markers with the pad nets in the `.kicad_pcb` (rotated 180°: DIN top-left,
  GND top-right, 3V3 bottom-left, DOUT bottom-right).
- Most of the price is per order (setup, stencil, a feeder fee per Extended part, X-ray), so 5 boards
  cost little more than 2. Customs description: DIY Hobby Circuit Board. UK VAT is charged at checkout.
- The module's antenna **overhangs the board edge by 5.05 mm on purpose** (marked on Dwgs.User).

## Cable

J1 is a JST **SH 1.0 mm** 5-pin socket: 1 VBUS, 2 D−, 3 D+, 4 CC, 5 GND, the house USB connector
(below). The proper cable is LCSC Cable's custom one (quote requested 16 Oct, PO ref CRYPT-16): a bare
USB-C plug (C393939, no resistor in it) to JST SHR-05V-S-B with SSH-003T-P0.2, UL1571 28 AWG, 150 mm,
wired straight through (VBUS, D−, D+, CC on A5, GND; VCONN unconnected), so it works from any USB-C host
or charger. Off the shelf, a "USB to SH1.0 5P" cable in micro-USB order (VBUS, D−, D+, ID, GND; sold as
"forward wire order") fits as is, its unused ID on pin 4. It's USB-A, so a USB-C host needs a USB-C-to-A (OTG) adapter,
whose plug carries the 5.1 kΩ on CC. Otherwise splice a USB cable to an SH1.0 5-pin pigtail (pigtail
wire colours mean nothing: go by pin position; a USB-C plug needs CC wired to pin 4).

- **Check the cable's pinout with a meter before the first plug-in.** There is no reverse-polarity
  protection. (A breakout's ready-made JST lead turned out to be miswired, SDA on ground.)
- An inline USB power meter on the first plug-in shows 5 V arriving and the current the key draws.

### House USB connector

J1's layout is Martin's house standard for USB on his own boards (16 Oct 2026), so one cable fits them all:

1. **Connector:** JST SM05B-SRSS-TB (SH 1.0 mm, 5-pin, right angle): 1 VBUS, 2 D−, 3 D+, 4 CC, 5 GND,
   printed on the silkscreen.
2. **CC:** one 5.1 kΩ from pin 4 to GND on every board. The cable carries CC straight through, so the
   board's Rd is what makes a USB-C charger or a C-to-C host switch its 5 V on.
3. **Power:** 5 V only (no PD), about 0.5 A in normal use and 1 A at most (the SH crimp's rating). A
   hungrier board gets its own power connector.
4. **Data:** USB 2.0 Full Speed (an S3's native USB, or a USB-serial chip).
5. **Protection:** an ESD array on D+/D−, and on VBUS a polyfuse plus a TVS or reverse-polarity diode
   (this board has neither yet: add them on the next revision).

It stays 5-pin rather than the 4-pin SH order sold as "Type-C to SH1.0 4P" (decided 16 Oct 2026): a 4-pin
cable has to carry the 5.1 kΩ in its plug, and cheap ones often don't (they then work from USB-A only);
their pin orders vary anyway; and a 4-pin SH socket is also Qwiic/STEMMA QT, whose pin 1 is GND, so a USB
lead plugged into one puts 5 V on ground. A 5-pin socket takes neither by mistake.

## Bring-up (each new board, into prod)

Needs PR #17 (the ATECC firmware) merged. N is the key's number; add `key-N` to `prod` in
`infra/keys.json` and `pnpm run deploy-prod` first (it creates the Thing).

1. **Meter the cable** against J1 (1 VBUS, 2 D−, 3 D+, 4 CC or unconnected, 5 GND), and the board's
   3V3 to GND for a short, before the first plug-in. Plug in through the power meter: 5 V, tens of mA.
2. **Provisioner** (PCB pins, so no `BOARD`): hold BOOT, plug in (a blank module enumerates as
   USB-Serial-JTAG), then `KEYSTORE=atecc esp32s3/build.sh provisioner` and
   `esp32s3/flash-manual.sh --provisioner PORT`.
3. `esp32s3/tools/atecc_provision.py PORT info` (a blank 608B: unlocked), `lock` (type the serial),
   then `check` (not `check full`: that's for a sacrificial chip).
4. `atecc_provision.py PORT csr tinycrypt-prod-key-N FILE`, then
   `NPM_ENVIRONMENT=prod AWS_PROFILE=nakom.is-prod infra/scripts/provision-key.sh key-N --csr FILE`
   (`METRICS_URL=none` until home-servers' `vmauth_devices` has the key).
5. Key image: `NPM_ENVIRONMENT=prod KEYSTORE=atecc esp32s3/build.sh`, then
   `NPM_ENVIRONMENT=prod ESPTOOL_BEFORE=default_reset esp32s3/flash-manual.sh PORT tinycrypt-prod-key-N`.
   After this the port is the FIDO device; reflash by holding BOOT while plugging in.
6. Check it's online (it sends `version` on connect; Fleet prod lists it), enrol the watch (hold BOOT
   5 s, "Enrol with key"; a watch that knew this board before must forget it first), `hw_smoke.py`.
7. From then on, updates come from Fleet prod (CI publishes ATECC builds on merge once
   `FIRMWARE_KEYSTORE` is `atecc`).

## As built, against DESIGN.md

- **21 × 34.95 mm.** The board ends at the antenna boundary, so the antenna strip overhangs and
  no keep-out rule area is needed: there's no board under or beside the antenna. The 21 mm width gives
  the 2.8 mm strips beside the module room for the 3V3/EN parts and the debug pads.
- **Mounting holes are off the centre line**, which is the USB path: H1 just past the module, right of
  centre (under Mu's chest on the back, so its ring is masked there and her pixels over the drill are
  dropped); H2 beside J1, on the left.
- **Routing by hand** (`board/key_board.py`): the USB pair (0.2 mm, F.Cu, no stubs); the VBUS and 3V3
  trunks (0.45 mm); EN, RX, TX, SDA, SCL and CC in lanes. The only B.Cu under the USB pair is a
  1.75 mm VBUS hop: U3's VBUS pin sits between D- and D+, so reaching it means crossing one of them.
  3V3 reaches the right half under J1's body, and R2 up the right edge on B.Cu. No B.Cu track crosses
  Mu's face.
- **GND** is left to the pours: the router doesn't route it. Stitching vias sit on a 1 mm grid wherever
  they clear the copper, the silk text and Mu's white fur. A few hand-placed vias tie in pour pockets
  that the tracks close off, and four sit near the antenna-end edge.
- **U1** has a -0.18 mm paste margin on its nine centre pads (about 49 % coverage, against module
  lift) and no thermal vias. Its corner GND pads and U2's GND pin connect to the pour solidly.
- The **button** uses diagonal pads 2 and 3, which are on different contact bars whichever way the
  switch pairs its pins.
- **Parts within 1 mm of a long edge** (exceptions to the rail rule): R2, C6, R4, R5 and the debug
  pads in the right strip, and the mounting-hole rings.
- **22 Ω USB series resistors: not fitted.** They would break the straight hand-routed pair, and Full
  Speed USB doesn't need them.
